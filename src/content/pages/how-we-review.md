Our reviews are designed to answer one question: **should you spend your money and time on this?** This page explains how we reach our conclusions.

## Two kinds of review

**Hands-on reviews** are based on our own use of the product over a reasonable period. They describe what we observed, under what conditions, and what we could not test.

**Research-based reviews** assess a product using published specifications, official documentation, standards and other reliable sources — without hands-on use. They are useful for comparing options and understanding trade-offs, but they are clearly labelled, and they never carry a score.

Every review states which kind it is in its "At a glance" box.

## What we look at

The details depend on the product, but we consistently consider:

- **Core purpose** — how well it does the main job it is sold for.
- **Usability** — setup, everyday use, accessibility and documentation.
- **Build and longevity** — materials, repairability, software update commitments where published.
- **Privacy and security** — what data is collected and how it is protected, based on published policies.
- **Value** — what you get for the price compared with credible alternatives.
- **Who it suits** — and who should look elsewhere.

## Scores

Scores are optional and only appear on hands-on reviews. When used, they are out of 10 in half-point steps:

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 9–10 | Outstanding. Among the best available for its intended audience. |
| 7–8.5 | Very good. Recommended, with minor caveats. |
| 5–6.5 | Mixed. Suitable for some people; meaningful compromises. |
| 3–4.5 | Poor. Significant problems for most buyers. |
| 0–2.5 | Not recommended. |

A score is a summary, not a substitute for the review. We do not publish aggregate ratings, star ratings from readers, or scores for products we have not used.

## What we will never do

- Claim to have tested a product we have not used.
- Publish invented benchmark figures, quotes or specifications.
- Let a manufacturer, retailer or advertiser approve or change a review.
- Change a verdict because of an affiliate or advertising relationship.

## Updates

Products change through software updates and price changes. When we materially update a review, we change its "Updated" date and explain significant changes.
