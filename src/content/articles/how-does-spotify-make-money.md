Spotify is one of the most recognisable music platforms in the world, yet millions of people use it without paying a monthly subscription. They can listen to music, discover new artists and build playlists without entering their card details for a Premium plan.

That raises an obvious business question: if most users do not pay, how does Spotify make money?

The answer is that Spotify operates two connected businesses. It sells subscriptions to listeners who want a paid experience, while also generating advertising revenue from its free audience. The free tier is not simply a charitable alternative to Premium; it is part of the company's strategy for attracting users and converting some of them into paying customers.

However, Spotify's business is more complicated than charging for music. It must also pay royalties and other costs, negotiate with the music industry, invest in technology and persuade listeners that its paid plans are worth the money.

## 1. Premium subscriptions are the main revenue engine

Spotify's most straightforward source of income is Premium.

Subscribers pay for access to features included in their chosen plan. These can include listening without the advertising associated with the free tier, offline downloads and other benefits, depending on the plan and current product offering.

Spotify offers different subscription options, including individual and multi-user plans in supported markets. This allows it to serve different household arrangements and budgets.

The business model is attractive because subscription payments can recur every month. Recurring revenue helps Spotify forecast demand and invest in its service, although subscribers can cancel and revenue is affected by pricing, regional differences and changes in customer numbers.

Spotify's 2025 results illustrate the scale of this business. In its fourth-quarter earnings announcement, the company reported 290 million Premium subscribers worldwide and total quarterly revenue of approximately €4.5 billion.

Those figures describe Spotify's overall business at a particular point in time; they should not be interpreted as the number of paying subscribers or revenue generated in the UK alone.

## 2. Free users generate advertising revenue

Spotify's free tier provides another source of income.

Advertisers pay to reach audiences through supported advertising formats. Depending on the product and market, advertising can appear between music tracks or within podcasts and other content.

Spotify can offer advertisers access to an audience with particular listening habits and interests, subject to the platform's advertising and privacy policies.

This arrangement gives Spotify a reason to attract users who may never subscribe. Even if someone does not pay directly, their listening activity can create opportunities for advertising revenue.

However, an advertising-supported listener is not necessarily as valuable as a paying subscriber. Revenue depends on factors such as advertising demand, the number of ads served, audience engagement and the markets in which listeners are located.

The free tier therefore serves two purposes: it can generate advertising income directly and give people an opportunity to experience Spotify before deciding whether to subscribe.

## 3. Why doesn't Spotify make everyone pay?

A free tier helps Spotify compete for listeners who might otherwise choose another streaming service or avoid paying for music altogether.

If Spotify required every listener to subscribe, it could lose people who are unwilling to pay a monthly fee. That would reduce its potential advertising audience and limit opportunities to convert free users into customers.

The free experience also acts as a route into the paid product. Someone who builds playlists, follows artists and uses Spotify regularly may eventually decide that Premium's additional features justify the cost.

This is an example of a freemium business model: offer a useful free product, then charge for an enhanced experience.

The strategy is not unique to Spotify. Many software and online services use free access to build an audience, encourage regular use and sell paid plans to a proportion of their users.

## 4. How much money does Spotify actually make?

Revenue and profit are not the same thing.

Revenue measures the money generated by a company's activities. Profit is what remains after the relevant costs and expenses are accounted for.

Spotify must pay music rights holders under its licensing arrangements. It also incurs costs associated with technology infrastructure, product development, marketing, staff and other operations.

The relationship between revenue and profit is therefore central to understanding the business.

Spotify's fourth-quarter 2025 results reported total revenue of approximately €4.5 billion and operating income of €701 million. Those are historical company-reported figures, not a statement of its current quarterly performance in October 2026.

The figures illustrate an important point: generating billions in sales does not mean Spotify keeps all the money it receives.

A meaningful assessment of its performance requires looking at revenue growth, operating costs, margins, cash flow and the number of users and subscribers the business serves.

## 5. Why are music royalties such a big deal?

Spotify does not generally own all the music available on its platform. It licenses rights from music companies, publishers and other rights holders under arrangements that govern how content can be distributed and monetised.

When people stream music, Spotify's financial obligations are influenced by these licensing agreements and the relevant usage and revenue calculations.

This makes Spotify different from a conventional software company that can sell a digital product without paying a separate rights holder each time a customer uses particular content.

The platform must balance the value it provides to listeners and advertisers against the cost of licensing the catalogue that makes the service attractive.

The details vary by agreement and type of content, so it is misleading to assume that every stream generates a fixed payment or that all artists receive the same amount per play.

Spotify's business model depends on maintaining a catalogue listeners want while managing the economics of distributing that content at scale.

## 6. Does Spotify make money from podcasts and audiobooks?

Spotify has expanded beyond music streaming into podcasts, audiobooks and other audio-related experiences.

These areas can support the wider business in several ways. They may help attract and retain subscribers, create additional advertising opportunities and give users more reasons to spend time on the platform.

Podcasts can support advertising and sponsorship arrangements, while audiobook access and related features can form part of certain subscription offerings or be available under separate terms, depending on the market.

However, diversification does not automatically make every content category profitable. Production costs, licensing arrangements, creator payments and audience demand all affect the economics.

The important strategic idea is that Spotify wants to become more than a place where people press play on individual songs. A broader audio platform can give listeners more reasons to remain engaged and pay for a subscription.

## 7. Why does Spotify keep raising prices?

Subscription pricing helps determine how much revenue Spotify generates from its paying audience.

When a company increases prices, it may generate more revenue per subscriber, assuming customers remain subscribed. But higher prices can also encourage cancellations or persuade some users to return to the free tier.

Spotify therefore has to balance price increases against customer retention, competition and the value listeners perceive in its service.

Price changes may also vary between countries and subscription plans. A UK subscriber may face a different price from someone in another market, reflecting local conditions and the company's pricing decisions.

To assess the impact of a price increase, it is useful to consider subscriber growth, cancellation behaviour, revenue per subscriber and operating profitability together.

## 8. Is Spotify's business model sustainable?

Spotify benefits from recurring subscriptions, a large audience and a service that can distribute digital content globally. These characteristics create opportunities to grow revenue without building a separate physical product for each customer.

However, the business faces several challenges.

- **Licensing costs:** Music rights and other content arrangements represent significant costs.
- **Competition:** Listeners can choose other streaming services or alternative entertainment.
- **Subscriber retention:** Customers may cancel if prices rise or the product becomes less attractive.
- **Advertising demand:** The free tier's economics depend partly on advertisers and market conditions.
- **Content strategy:** Expanding into additional audio formats introduces new opportunities and costs.

The scale of Spotify's audience is valuable, but audience size alone does not guarantee profitability. The company must continue turning engagement into revenue while managing the costs of delivering its service.

## The bottom line

Spotify makes money because it does not depend on every listener paying a subscription.

Premium subscribers provide recurring revenue, while free users create opportunities for advertising income and future subscription growth. The two tiers reinforce one another by allowing Spotify to reach a broad audience while offering a paid upgrade for people who want additional features.

The business still has substantial costs, particularly those associated with music rights, technology and operations. Revenue is therefore not the same as profit, and a large audience does not automatically guarantee strong financial performance.

Spotify's strategy is to attract listeners, keep them engaged and monetise that engagement through subscriptions and advertising.

That is why the free tier can be commercially valuable even when a user never pays a penny. It helps make Spotify a larger platform, supports advertising revenue and creates a path towards Premium for those who eventually decide the upgrade is worth it.
