Nothing's newest phone is not the Phone (4) many people expected. Instead, the London-based company announced the Phone (4b) on 7 July 2026, the first handset in a new (b) series that Nothing describes as an entry point to its ecosystem. It sits below the Phone (4a) and Phone (4a) Pro, costs £299 in the UK, and borrows design ideas from both of its more expensive siblings. This explainer covers what it is, what it offers and where the compromises are.

## Why there is no Phone (4) this year

Earlier in 2026, Nothing chief executive Carl Pei said the company would not release a new flagship this year, and that the Phone (3) would remain its top model throughout 2026. The company's argument was that it did not want to produce a new flagship every year simply for the sake of it.

That leaves 2026's new phones as the Phone (4a) and Phone (4a) Pro, launched in March, and now the Phone (4b). If you are waiting for a true successor to the Phone (3), it has not been announced.

## What the Phone (4b) is

The Phone (4b) is Nothing's attempt to bring its recognisable design to a lower price. According to Nothing, it combines two elements from the (4a) series:

- **The Glyph Bar** introduced on the Phone (4a), here in a simplified form with 45 individually controlled mini-LEDs
- **A unibody design** inspired by the Phone (4a) Pro, though the (4b) uses polycarbonate rather than metal

It is available in black, white and blue. In India, there is also a limited RCB Edition made with the Royal Challengers Bengaluru cricket team, sold only through Nothing's Bengaluru store.

## Key specifications

### Display and performance

- 6.77-inch AMOLED display, 2,344 x 1,080 resolution, 120Hz refresh rate
- Qualcomm Snapdragon 6 Gen 4, a lower mid-range 4nm chip
- 8GB of RAM
- 128GB of storage in the UK and Europe

The Snapdragon 6 Gen 4 is a step below the chips in the (4a) series. It should be adequate for everyday use, but buyers who play demanding games or want the most headroom over several years may prefer something faster.

### Battery and charging

The battery is the headline feature, but it depends on where you buy:

- **5,200mAh** in the UK, Europe and most markets
- **6,000mAh** in India

Even the 5,200mAh version is the largest Nothing has fitted outside India. Charging is 33W wired, which Nothing says reaches 50% in under 30 minutes. Nothing also claims the battery will retain up to 90% of its original capacity after 1,200 charge cycles. That is a manufacturer figure and has not been independently verified.

### Cameras

- 50MP main camera with optical image stabilisation
- 8MP ultra-wide camera
- 16MP front camera
- Video up to 4K at 30fps

There is no telephoto lens, which is one of the clearer differences from the Phone (4a).

### Durability and software

The Phone (4b) has an IP64 rating, meaning it is protected against dust and water splashes but is not designed for submersion. It ships with Nothing OS 4.1 on Android 16, with a promise of three years of Android updates and six years of security updates.

## How it compares with the rest of the range

| | Phone (4b) | Phone (4a) | Phone (4a) Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK starting price | £299 | £379 | £519 |
| Chip | Snapdragon 6 Gen 4 | Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 | Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 |
| Display | 6.77in, 120Hz | 6.78in, 120Hz | 6.83in, 144Hz |
| Battery (UK) | 5,200mAh | 5,080mAh | 5,080mAh |
| Rear cameras | Two | Three, incl. telephoto | Three |
| Body | Polycarbonate unibody | Glass back | Aluminium unibody |

The pattern is straightforward. The (4b) gives up processing power, a third camera and premium materials, and in exchange gets a slightly bigger battery and a noticeably lower price.

## Availability and the US question

In the UK, the Phone (4b) went on sale first at Nothing's London store on 11 July, with wider shipping from 17 July. It costs £299 in the UK and €329 in Europe for the 8GB and 128GB model.

It is not being sold in the US. Readers there will need to look at the Phone (4a) Pro, which is the only (4a) model Nothing sells in America.

## Who it suits

The Phone (4b) makes most sense for:

- People who want Nothing's design and software without paying (4a) prices
- Users who prioritise battery life over camera versatility
- Anyone who values a long security-update promise at this price

It is a weaker fit for:

- Mobile gamers or heavy multitaskers
- Photographers who want optical zoom
- Anyone in the US

## The bottom line

The Phone (4b) is not a flagship and does not pretend to be one. It is a sensible, design-led mid-range phone that trades some performance and camera flexibility for a lower price and a large battery. Its software support commitment is solid for the money. If you like Nothing's approach but find the Phone (4a) a little too expensive, it is worth a look; if you want the best camera or the most power, the (4a) series or a rival will serve you better.
