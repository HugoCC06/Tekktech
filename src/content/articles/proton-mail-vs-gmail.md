Email is one of the few digital services people can use every day for years without seriously reconsidering their choice. Gmail is familiar, widely integrated and convenient, while Proton Mail has built its reputation around privacy and encrypted communication.

Both services can handle ordinary personal email, but they approach the experience differently. Gmail benefits from Google's wider ecosystem, including Drive, Calendar and other connected services. Proton Mail places greater emphasis on encryption, data protection and tools designed to limit exposure of private information.

Switching is not automatically an upgrade. It depends on how much you value privacy, which services you rely on and whether you are willing to adjust your workflow.

**Proton Mail is worth considering if privacy is a major priority. Gmail remains compelling if convenience, integrations and an established workflow matter more.**

## Proton Mail vs Gmail: At a glance

| Feature | Proton Mail | Gmail |

| Main appeal | Privacy and encrypted email | Search, convenience and Google integration |
| Encryption | End-to-end encryption for supported communications and zero-access storage for supported data | Encryption in transit and at rest; standard Gmail does not provide end-to-end encryption by default |
| Free account | Available, with limits on storage and features | Available, with storage shared across Google services |

Plans, storage limits and feature availability change over time. Check the providers' current UK offerings before making a decision based on a particular allowance or price.

## 1. Privacy and encryption

Privacy is the clearest reason to consider Proton Mail.

Proton uses end-to-end encryption for supported communications and zero-access encryption for supported stored message content. This is designed to prevent Proton from reading encrypted message content under the relevant circumstances.

However, encryption does not make every part of email invisible. Email relies on protocols that expose some information, such as sender and recipient addresses, to deliver messages. Messages exchanged with ordinary email providers are not automatically end-to-end encrypted simply because one participant uses Proton Mail.

Gmail protects messages with encryption in transit and at rest within its systems. Standard Gmail does not provide end-to-end encryption by default for ordinary personal email.

Google also processes information to provide features such as spam detection, Smart Reply and inbox organisation, subject to its policies and settings. This does not mean that human employees routinely read private emails, nor does it mean that Google sells personal information. It does mean the services have different technical approaches to message processing.

If you regularly exchange sensitive information and want stronger protection for message content, Proton Mail has a meaningful advantage. You should still consider recipient compatibility, metadata exposure and account security.

## 2. Security: Which is safer?

Security involves more than encryption.

Both services provide security features intended to protect accounts and reduce exposure to spam, phishing and other threats. Gmail benefits from Google's extensive security infrastructure and established anti-abuse systems. Proton offers encryption-focused protections and account security controls.

Neither service eliminates the risk of account compromise. A weak password, stolen session or successful phishing attack can undermine an otherwise strong setup.

Regardless of provider, enable multifactor authentication, use a unique password and keep recovery information secure. Be cautious with unexpected attachments, login requests and links, even when an email appears to come from someone you know.

Proton's encryption is especially relevant to confidentiality. Gmail's integrated security and spam filtering may be particularly attractive to people who value a convenient, managed inbox. The better choice depends on the threat you are trying to reduce.

## 3. Everyday usability and search

Gmail is familiar to many users and offers powerful search, labels, filters, categories and integrations. If you already organise work through Google Calendar, Drive and Docs, keeping email in the same ecosystem can reduce friction.

Proton Mail provides familiar email features too, including folders, filters and search tools. However, encryption affects how some search features operate, and functionality may differ depending on the client, message type and account configuration.

Neither interface is inherently right for everyone. The important question is whether the service supports the way you manage email.

If your inbox contains years of correspondence and you rely heavily on advanced searches or integrations, test Proton Mail with your actual workflow before moving everything.

## 4. Storage and pricing

Both providers offer free accounts, but the available storage and features differ.

Gmail storage is shared across eligible Google services, including Google Drive and Google Photos. This is convenient if you use Google's ecosystem, but attachments and files stored elsewhere can reduce the space available to email.

Proton Mail offers limited free storage and additional capacity through paid plans. Its wider paid ecosystem may also appeal to users who want email alongside other privacy-focused services.

Pricing and allowances change, so compare current UK plan details rather than relying on old figures. Consider whether you need a custom domain, additional addresses, more storage or features such as expanded calendar and file-storage services.

## 5. Can you switch without losing your emails?

Moving providers does not necessarily require abandoning your existing messages.

Proton offers migration tools for supported accounts, while Gmail supports several methods for importing or forwarding email. The precise process depends on the services involved and the features currently available.

Before migrating, check what will transfer:

- Existing messages and attachments.
- Contacts and address books.
- Calendar events.
- Labels, folders and filters.
- Aliases and custom-domain settings.
- Automatic forwarding and connected applications.

Do not assume that importing messages will reproduce your existing setup perfectly. Some rules, labels and integrations may need to be recreated manually.

A gradual transition is often easier. Set up Proton Mail, import your existing messages where supported and test important accounts before changing your primary email address everywhere.

Keep your old account accessible during the transition, especially if it is used for account recovery, banking, university or work.

## 6. Which is better for work and university?

For students and professionals already using Google Workspace, Gmail can be difficult to replace. Shared documents, calendars, collaboration tools and institutional accounts may depend on Google's services.

Proton Mail can be attractive for personal correspondence, independent projects and users who want a clearer separation between their private email and wider productivity ecosystem.

A mixed approach is also possible. You can retain Gmail for services that depend on it while using Proton Mail for selected personal communications.

If your email address is managed by a university or employer, you may not have permission to change the underlying provider. Check the organisation's account policies before planning a migration.

## The bottom line

Proton Mail and Gmail solve the same basic problem but prioritise different things.

Proton Mail is worth considering if encrypted communication, privacy-focused design and reduced exposure of message content are important to you. Gmail is a strong choice if you rely on Google's services, value powerful search and prefer a familiar, integrated workflow.

Switching makes the most sense when your priorities have changed, not simply because one provider has a stronger reputation in a particular area.

If you are unsure, try Proton Mail alongside Gmail before migrating completely. Review the current plan limits, test your essential workflows and move gradually.

The best email provider is the one that meets your privacy needs while remaining practical enough for you to use securely every day.
