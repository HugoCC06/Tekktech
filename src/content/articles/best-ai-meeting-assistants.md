Meetings create a surprising amount of work after the conversation ends. Someone has to write notes, identify decisions, assign tasks and send follow-up messages. When a team holds several meetings each day, these small administrative jobs can consume hours that would be better spent doing actual work.

AI meeting assistants aim to reduce that burden. They can transcribe conversations, generate summaries, identify potential action items and make it easier to revisit what was discussed.

But there is a difference between a tool that produces an impressive-looking summary and one that genuinely saves time. Accuracy, integrations, privacy and the amount of manual correction required all matter.

**For most people, the best AI meeting assistant is the one that fits their existing meeting workflow and produces reliable notes with minimal editing.**

## The best AI meeting assistants compared

| Tool | Main appeal | Best suited to |
|---|---|---|
| Otter.ai | Transcription and searchable meeting records | People who regularly need transcripts |
| Fireflies.ai | Meeting summaries and workflow integrations | Teams managing recurring meetings |
| Fathom | Meeting recording, summaries and follow-ups | Professionals who want straightforward meeting notes |
| Granola | AI-enhanced notes built around your own meeting notes | People who prefer a notes-focused workflow |
| Microsoft Teams with Copilot | AI assistance integrated into Microsoft's collaboration environment | Organisations already using Microsoft 365 |

Features, plan limits and integrations vary by subscription and may change. The table describes each product's general positioning rather than claiming that one tool wins every category.

## 1. Otter.ai: Best for searchable transcripts

Otter.ai focuses on recording conversations, generating transcripts and summarising meetings.

Its searchable transcripts can be useful when you need to recover a specific detail from a long discussion rather than relying on memory or manually searching through notes.

This is particularly valuable for interviews, research discussions, lectures and recurring team meetings. The usefulness depends on transcription accuracy, speaker identification and how clearly participants speak.

The main limitation is that a transcript is not automatically a reliable record of every decision. Background noise, technical vocabulary and overlapping speech can cause errors, while summaries can omit context.

**Choose Otter.ai if** you frequently revisit conversations and want searchable records alongside summaries.

## 2. Fireflies.ai: Best for connected workflows

Fireflies.ai offers transcription, meeting summaries and tools for extracting information from conversations. It also promotes integrations with other business applications, allowing meeting information to feed into wider workflows.

This can be valuable for sales teams, project managers and people who need to transfer meeting outcomes into customer records or task-management systems.

The real benefit comes from reducing duplicated work. If meeting notes can be routed into the tools your team already uses, fewer details need to be copied manually.

However, more integrations also introduce more configuration and privacy considerations. Check which applications are connected, what information is transferred and who can access the resulting records.

**Choose Fireflies.ai if** your main problem is turning recurring meetings into organised, searchable information and follow-up workflows.

## 3. Fathom: Best for straightforward meeting summaries

Fathom focuses on capturing meetings and producing summaries, with features intended to make reviewing conversations and sharing key information easier.

It can be useful when you want to participate in a meeting without simultaneously trying to record every detail.

For regular internal meetings or customer conversations, automatically generated summaries can provide a convenient starting point for follow-up messages and task lists.

As with any AI meeting assistant, the quality of the result depends on the recording, conversation and selected features. You should review important names, commitments, figures and deadlines before sharing a summary.

**Choose Fathom if** you want a meeting-focused tool that helps you review conversations and prepare follow-ups without building a complicated workflow.

## 4. Granola: Best for a notes-focused approach

Granola takes a different approach to meeting notes, combining information from conversations with the user's own notes.

That can appeal to people who prefer to remain in control of what they record while using AI to organise and expand their notes afterwards.

The approach is particularly useful when a fully automated transcript is not the main objective. Instead, the goal is to turn rough notes and meeting context into a clearer record.

Check the current supported platforms and meeting-capture methods before choosing it. Compatibility and available features can differ from those of tools designed primarily around automated recording.

**Choose Granola if** you prefer an AI-assisted notes workflow rather than relying entirely on a generated transcript.

## 5. Microsoft Teams with Copilot: Best for Microsoft 365 users

For organisations already using Microsoft Teams and Microsoft 365, built-in AI features can be attractive because they sit within an existing collaboration environment.

Depending on the plan, configuration and meeting setup, supported features may help summarise discussions, identify decisions and retrieve information from meetings.

The advantage is integration. Organisations may be able to use their existing identity management, access controls and collaboration tools instead of introducing another standalone service.

The trade-off is that availability and cost depend on the organisation's Microsoft licensing and settings. IT administrators may also restrict which features can be used.

**Choose Teams with Copilot if** your organisation already relies on Microsoft 365 and wants to keep meeting assistance within its existing environment.

## Which features actually save time?

The most useful features are not necessarily the most impressive demonstrations.

### Automatic summaries

A good summary reduces the need to review an entire recording. It should identify the main topics, decisions and unresolved questions without inventing conclusions.

### Action-item extraction

Identifying tasks, owners and deadlines can prevent follow-up work from being forgotten. However, AI-generated assignments should be checked against what participants actually agreed.

### Searchable meeting history

Search becomes increasingly valuable when a team has dozens or hundreds of recorded conversations. Being able to find an earlier decision can save time and reduce repeated discussions.

### Integrations

Integrations can eliminate copying and pasting, but only when they fit the existing workflow. A long list of supported applications is less useful than one reliable integration with the task manager or customer system your team actually uses.

## Privacy, consent and accuracy

Meeting assistants can process sensitive information, including customer details, financial discussions and internal plans.

Before enabling one, check how recordings and transcripts are stored, who can access them, whether they are used to train models and how deletion works. Review organisational policies and obtain appropriate consent from participants.

In the UK, recording and processing conversations can raise data protection and confidentiality obligations. Requirements depend on the context, purpose and information involved.

Do not assume that a meeting assistant is compliant simply because it offers business features. Organisations should assess the specific service, configuration and intended use.

## The bottom line

AI meeting assistants can reduce administrative work, but the greatest benefits come from reliable summaries, accurate action items and integrations that fit your workflow.

Otter.ai is worth considering for searchable transcripts, Fireflies.ai for connected workflows, Fathom for straightforward meeting summaries, Granola for an AI-assisted notes approach and Teams with Copilot for organisations already using Microsoft's ecosystem.

Before paying, test a representative meeting and measure how much editing the output requires. Include setup time, subscription costs and privacy considerations in the decision.

A useful assistant should leave you with less work after the meeting, not simply another document that needs checking.
