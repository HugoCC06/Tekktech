Notion and Obsidian are two of the most popular apps for people trying to get their lives in order, from tasks and goals to reading notes and long-term projects. They are often compared, but they are built on very different ideas. Notion is a cloud-based workspace built around databases and pages. Obsidian is a note-taking app that stores plain text files on your own device. Neither is better in the abstract; the right choice depends on how you think and what you want to organise.

## The core difference

### Notion: structured and cloud-based

Notion treats everything as blocks on pages, and its standout feature is databases. A database can be shown as a table, a board, a calendar, a list or a timeline, and the same information can be filtered and sorted in different ways. That makes it well suited to things with clear structure:

- Habit trackers and task lists
- Budgets, reading lists and travel plans
- Project dashboards with deadlines and statuses
- Shared plans with a partner, family or housemates

Your data lives on Notion's servers, which makes syncing and sharing straightforward.

### Obsidian: linked and local

Obsidian stores your notes as Markdown files in a folder on your device, which it calls a vault. You connect ideas by linking notes to each other, and the app shows backlinks and a graph view of how your notes relate. It suits:

- Journaling and reflection
- Study notes and learning
- Building a long-term personal knowledge base, often called a "second brain"
- Writing and thinking that grows over time

Because your notes are ordinary files, you can open them in other apps, back them up however you like, and keep them even if you stop using Obsidian.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Notion | Obsidian |
|---|---|---|
| Where data lives | Notion's cloud | Your device, as Markdown files |
| Best at | Structured information and databases | Linked notes and long-form thinking |
| Offline use | Supported in the apps, with limits | Works fully offline by design |
| Collaboration | Built in | Limited; designed mainly for individuals |
| Customisation | Templates and page layouts | Themes and a large range of community plugins |
| Learning curve | Moderate; databases take practice | Moderate; plugins and linking take practice |
| Personal use cost | Free plan available | Core app free |

## Offline use and reliability

This used to be a clear win for Obsidian, and it still is, though the gap has narrowed.

In August 2025, Notion added offline mode to its desktop and mobile apps. You can view, edit and create pages without a connection, and changes sync when you reconnect. However, there are limits:

- Pages must be marked as available offline individually on each device, although paid plans also download recent and favourited pages automatically
- Offline mode does not work in a web browser
- Features that rely on live data, such as embeds and forms, are unavailable offline

Obsidian, by contrast, works entirely offline by default because your notes are already on your device. If you often work on trains, planes or with patchy internet, that matters.

## Privacy and ownership

If you want full control over your data, Obsidian has the advantage. Your notes stay on your device unless you choose to sync them. If you pay for Obsidian Sync, Obsidian says your data is protected with end-to-end encryption.

Notion stores your content on its servers. For most personal uses that is fine, and it is what makes sharing and syncing easy. But if you plan to keep sensitive journals or personal records, consider whether you are comfortable with that.

## Pricing

Both apps can be used for free, but costs work differently.

**Notion** has a free plan for individuals. Its paid plans are aimed at teams: Plus is listed at $10 per member per month billed annually, and Business at $20. Full access to Notion AI is now tied to the Business plan, with only a limited trial on Free and Plus. Notion localises prices, so UK prices may differ.

**Obsidian's** core app is free for personal use. You pay only for optional services:

- **Obsidian Sync**, to sync notes across devices with encryption
- **Obsidian Publish**, to publish notes as a website

You can also sync your vault yourself using a cloud storage service, but that takes more setup and can cause conflicts. Obsidian offers a 40% discount on Sync and Publish for students, faculty and non-profit employees. Check Obsidian's pricing page for current prices and commercial-use terms.

## Which should you choose?

### Choose Notion if you:

- Like dashboards, tables and visual organisation
- Want to share plans or trackers with other people
- Prefer something that syncs everywhere with no setup
- Want templates you can adapt quickly

### Choose Obsidian if you:

- Mainly write notes, journal or study
- Care about privacy and owning your files
- Work offline often
- Enjoy customising your tools with plugins

### Or use both

Plenty of people use Notion for structured planning, such as tasks, budgets and shared projects, and Obsidian for private notes and thinking. This adds a little overhead, but it plays to each app's strengths.

## The bottom line

Notion is the better choice if organising your life means tracking things: tasks, habits, goals and plans, especially ones you share with others. Obsidian is the better choice if it means capturing and connecting ideas in a private, offline-first space that you fully control. Both are free to start, so the most reliable way to decide is to spend a week setting up one real area of your life in each and see which you keep returning to.
