A smart home does not need dozens of expensive gadgets, complicated installations or a wall full of touchscreens. In fact, the most useful upgrades are often the simplest: lights that switch on automatically, plugs that turn ordinary appliances into remotely controlled devices, and sensors that tell you when something needs attention.

The challenge is knowing where to start. Smart home products are sold across multiple ecosystems, and not every device works with every assistant, hub or automation platform. Some also require subscriptions or additional hardware that increases the real cost.

The best approach is to buy devices that solve everyday problems first, then expand your setup as you discover what is genuinely useful.

Here are 10 smart home gadgets worth considering before spending money on more specialised technology.

## 1. Smart plugs

Smart plugs are among the easiest ways to begin building a connected home. They allow you to control compatible plug-in appliances remotely or through schedules and automations.

Useful examples include lamps, seasonal lighting and certain small appliances that are safe to switch on and off through a plug.

Look for scheduling, energy monitoring if you need it, and compatibility with your preferred smart home platform. Matter support can make integration easier across compatible ecosystems.

One important limitation: a smart plug does not make every appliance smart. Devices that require a physical button press after power is restored may not behave as expected, and high-power appliances must remain within the plug's electrical rating.

## 2. Smart light bulbs

Smart bulbs provide a straightforward upgrade for bedrooms, living rooms and desk setups. Depending on the model, you can adjust brightness, change colour temperature, choose colours and schedule lights to turn on automatically.

They are particularly useful for routines such as gradually brightening a room in the morning or switching off lights at bedtime.

Before buying, check the bulb fitting, brightness, dimming support and whether you need a hub. Remember that a smart bulb usually needs its wall switch left on to remain connected.

## 3. Smart motion sensors

Motion sensors can trigger lights, notifications and other automations without requiring you to reach for your phone.

For example, a hallway light could turn on when movement is detected at night, then switch off after a period without activity.

Some sensors detect only motion, while others can also measure light levels or other environmental conditions. Choose based on the automation you want rather than buying the most feature-packed model.

Placement matters. Pets, direct sunlight and the sensor's field of view can affect behaviour, so expect some adjustment during setup.

## 4. Smart temperature and humidity sensors

A temperature and humidity sensor can help you understand conditions in different rooms.

It may reveal that a bedroom becomes uncomfortable overnight or that a bathroom stays humid after showering. Compatible systems can use these readings to trigger notifications, ventilation or other automations.

Sensors do not automatically improve indoor air quality, and temperature readings alone cannot identify every damp or mould problem. They are monitoring tools, not substitutes for appropriate ventilation or professional assessment where necessary.

## 5. A smart speaker or home hub

A compatible smart speaker or hub gives you a convenient way to control multiple devices through voice commands and routines.

Apple Home, Google Home and Amazon Alexa each support different combinations of accessories and features. Matter can improve compatibility, but it does not guarantee that every product or advanced function works on every platform.

Before purchasing a hub, check whether your existing phone, speaker or television already provides the necessary functionality. Some devices also need a Thread border router or a manufacturer-specific bridge.

## 6. A smart doorbell

A video doorbell can show who is at your front door and, depending on the model, provide motion alerts, two-way audio and recorded footage.

It can be useful when deliveries arrive while you are out or when you want to see visitors without opening the door.

Check whether the doorbell is battery-powered or wired, how recordings are stored and whether a subscription is required for particular features. Also consider Wi-Fi coverage, installation restrictions and privacy obligations when recording areas beyond your property.

## 7. A smart security camera

Indoor or outdoor cameras can provide remote viewing and motion notifications. Some offer local storage, object detection, night vision or integration with a broader security system.

Start by identifying the area you want to monitor and the features that matter. An indoor camera, for example, may not need the weather resistance required for outdoor installation.

Pay particular attention to account security, software updates and recording arrangements. A camera that depends entirely on a paid cloud service may become less useful if you stop subscribing.

## 8. Smart radiator or heating controls

Heating controls can make it easier to manage temperatures and schedules, particularly in homes where rooms are occupied at different times.

Smart radiator valves and thermostats may allow room-level scheduling, remote adjustments and automations based on temperature or occupancy.

Compatibility is critical. Check your existing boiler, thermostat, radiator valves and heating system before buying. Not every smart valve works with every installation, and potential energy savings depend on your existing setup, heating habits and how effectively the controls are used.

## 9. A smart energy monitor

Energy monitoring helps you understand electricity consumption rather than relying entirely on estimates.

Depending on the product, monitoring may take place through a compatible smart plug, a dedicated meter or a professionally installed monitoring device.

This can help identify energy-intensive appliances and reveal when consumption rises unexpectedly. However, monitoring does not guarantee lower bills: savings require changes to how energy is used.

In the UK, check compatibility with your meter and electrical installation before purchasing a whole-home monitor.

## 10. Water leak sensors

Water leak sensors are among the more practical smart home devices because they can alert you to a problem that might otherwise go unnoticed.

Place suitable sensors near washing machines, sinks, water heaters or other locations where leaks are possible. Some models provide local alarms, while connected versions can send notifications to your phone.

A sensor does not prevent a leak by itself. More advanced systems can connect to automatic shut-off valves, but these require compatible equipment and appropriate installation.

## What should you buy first?

Your first purchase should address a problem you encounter regularly.

| Your priority | Sensible starting point |
|---|---|
| Convenience | Smart plug |
| Better lighting | Smart bulbs |
| Automatic routines | Motion sensor |
| Home security | Video doorbell or camera |
| Heating control | Compatible smart thermostat or radiator valve |
| Preventing unnoticed leaks | Water leak sensor |
| Understanding electricity use | Energy monitor |

For most beginners, smart plugs and bulbs are good starting points because they are relatively simple to install and easy to understand. More complex products, particularly heating controls and security equipment, deserve additional compatibility checks.

## The bottom line

The best smart home is not necessarily the one with the most devices. It is the one that makes everyday tasks easier without introducing unnecessary complexity.

Start with a smart plug or bulb, choose a compatible platform, and add sensors or security products when you have a clear reason to use them. Check Matter support, hub requirements, subscription costs and installation constraints before buying.

A small, reliable system is more useful than a collection of expensive gadgets that rarely get used.
